\begin{abstract}
\noindent 
%In emerging-market patent offices, domestic firms' patents are less likely to be renewed relative to foreign-owned patents than in advanced economies

\noindent Emerging economies now account for a large share of the world's patenting, yet their patents are widely
thought to be worth less. This paper asks whether the technology is weaker (the invention) or whether domestic owners
lack the finance or capacity to use valuable patents (the inventor). Using renewal records for 3 million firm and
university patents filed in 2000--2005 in five emerging and five advanced economies, I compare domestic and foreign
owners within each office. At equal citations and technology field, the extra domestic shortfall in emerging markets
disappears: emerging-market firms keep their patents as long as or longer than comparable firms in rich economies,
with small Russian and, more weakly, Taiwanese firms the exceptions. Three tests find no sign of owner constraints:
owners keep the home patent of an invention longer than its foreign patents, credit crunches do not raise their
lapses, and fee cuts barely change renewal. Two gaps remain. Patents sold by small emerging-market firms survive much
longer after the sale. Chinese university patents are worth less than half as much as comparable foreign ones but
survive far longer once sold to firms. Renewal-model counterfactuals put the returns at stake in markets for
technology at up to \$1.2 billion per cohort in China, against almost nothing for fee relief.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} patent renewal; patent value; emerging markets; innovation; intellectual property;
markets for technology\\
\noindent\textbf{JEL codes:} O34, O31, O32, O57, L26
\end{abstract}

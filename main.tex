\begin{abstract}
\noindent Emerging economies now account for a large share of the world's patenting, yet their patents are widely
thought to be worth less. This paper asks whether the technology is weaker (the invention) or whether domestic owners
lack the finance or capacity to use valuable patents (the inventor). Using renewal records for 2.6 million firm and
university patents filed in 2000--2005 in five emerging and five advanced economies, I compare domestic and foreign
owners within each office. At equal citations, technology field and grant lag, the extra domestic shortfall in
emerging markets disappears: emerging-market firms keep their patents about as long as comparable firms in rich
economies, with Russia the exception. Three tests look for owner constraints. Two find none: owners keep the home
patent of an invention longer than its foreign patents, and credit crunches do not raise their lapses. The third
points to owner capacity at the margin: patents that small Chinese firms sell to unrelated buyers survive much longer
afterwards. Chinese university patents are the clear weak spot: they lapse sooner and are worth less than half as
much as comparable foreign ones, but survive far longer once sold to firms. Simulations with a renewal model put the
returns at stake in markets for technology and in university patenting in the hundreds of millions of dollars per
cohort in China, against under a million for fee relief.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} patent renewal; patent value; emerging markets; innovation; intellectual property;
markets for technology\\

\end{abstract}

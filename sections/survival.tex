\section{Survival at equal quality}\label{sec:survival}

From here on, survival is measured as being alive eight years after grant, for firms and universities only.

\paragraph{Main estimates.} Without controls, the extra domestic gap in the emerging-market offices is $-11.5$
points (SE 1.7): domestic firms' patents there are much less cited than foreign ones (by a factor of five in
China and six in Korea). With citations, field and owner-group controls it becomes $+4.3$ (1.5). Panel~A of
Table~\ref{tab:survival} shows that the result holds for every firm size and in China and Korea separately; Brazil
has too few domestic firm patents to estimate. Small firms in the emerging-market offices keep their patents
3.4~points more often than small firms in the rich-economy offices, relative to foreign owners in the same office.
Universities are the exception, at $-11.5$ (3.1).

\paragraph{Same invention.} Inventions patented both at home and abroad hold the technology fixed. With patent-family
fixed effects, small firms keep the home patent relatively longer than larger firms do, compared with the same
invention's foreign patents, in every origin country: China $+9.2$ (3.9), Korea $+10.0$ (2.0), Japan $+15.3$ (1.6),
the US $+4.2$ (1.3), Germany $+2.1$ (2.7) and the UK $+2.6$ (2.9). Small owners everywhere economise on the
expensive foreign patent first; there is no sign that small emerging-market firms find their home patent harder to
use.

\paragraph{Credit shocks.} If finance were binding, credit crunches should raise small domestic firms' lapses. In a
patent-year panel with shock years for each country (2008--09 in the rich economies; 2003--04 and 2008--09 in Korea;
2013 and 2017--18 in China; 2008--09 and 2015--16 in Brazil), the triple interaction of shock, small owner and
domestic owner is $+0.17$ points per year (0.91) in the emerging-market offices and $+0.21$ (0.58) in the
rich-economy ones (Panel~B).

\paragraph{Fees.} Fee changes give a direct test of cash constraints at renewal time. China's 85\% fee reduction
for small owners, whose window was extended in 2016 and 2018, lowers small firms' lapse rate only slightly, an
implied elasticity of 0.07 (intent to treat; take-up is not observed). Small US firms respond to stage-specific fee
changes with an elasticity of about 0.18; Korea's discounts are not identified. Small emerging-market firms are not
more fee-sensitive than small US firms.

\paragraph{Sales and licences.} Two results point to owner capacity at the margin. In a landmark design (patents
alive and not yet sold at ages 4--8; sold in the next year or not; outcome alive five years later, with citations
counted only up to the landmark), a sale raises survival by 4.6~points (0.7) on average, by a further 8.9 (1.0) for
small emerging-market firms and by only 0.3 (1.1) for small rich-economy firms. In China, a sale raises survival by
13.0 (0.7) for small domestic firms against 8.0 (1.0) for foreign owners, and recorded licences help small domestic
and foreign owners by a similar 13~points.

\section{Survival at equal quality}\label{sec:survival}

\paragraph{Main estimates.} Table~\ref{tab:survival} reports the extra domestic gap in emerging-market offices,
$\delta$ in equation~\eqref{eq:did}. Without quality controls it is $-10.1$ (1.5) points: domestic firms' patents in
emerging-market offices are much less cited than foreign ones. With citation slopes, field and owner-group effects it
becomes $+4.3$ (1.5). The estimate is the same for the original core of offices (China and Korea against the four
largest advanced economies) and close to zero, $-0.5$ (1.9), when Korea and Taiwan, the two high-income members of
the emerging-market group, are left out. By owner group (panel B), emerging-market firms of every size are ahead of
advanced-economy firms relative to foreign owners in the same office: small firms by 1.7 (0.5) points and larger
firms by 8--9 points. Universities are behind, at $-5.7$ (3.6), and by 16 points without Korea and Taiwan.

\paragraph{Richer quality controls.} Citations are a noisy measure of quality. Adding citations that block later
claims, citations received by age four and office-specific slopes in the grant lag lowers the estimates for every
firm size by 2--3 points (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rich}). Small emerging-market firms then come out 1.6 (0.6) points
behind, and 3.0 (0.7) with family size, which itself partly reflects owners' capacity to file abroad; larger firms
remain 4--9 points ahead. Small firms thus keep their patents about as long as small firms in the advanced
economies, within a few points either way, and larger firms longer.

\paragraph{By country.} Figure~\ref{fig:country} compares each emerging-market office with the five advanced-economy
offices, by owner group. Chinese and Korean firms of every size are ahead: small firms by 4.0 (0.6) and 1.7 (1.1)
points, larger firms by 8--12 points. Czech firms are ahead by similar amounts, though with wide intervals. Russia is
the exception: small Russian firms are 5.9 (1.5) points behind, and Russian universities 11.9 (4.7). Small Taiwanese
firms are 3.8 (1.5) points behind, while large Taiwanese firms and universities are well ahead. Even the Russian and
Taiwanese shortfalls sit inside the range of domestic--foreign gaps among small firms in the advanced-economy
offices, from about zero in the United Kingdom to 18 points in Japan (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:within}).

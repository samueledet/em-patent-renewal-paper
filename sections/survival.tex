\section{Survival at equal quality}\label{sec:survival}

\paragraph{Main estimates.} Table~\ref{tab:survival} reports $\delta$ in equation~\eqref{eq:did}, the extra
domestic--foreign gap in emerging-market offices. Across the ten offices and for all firms and universities together,
without controls it is $-12.3$ points (column 1). Comparing patents with the same citations, technology field, owner
group and grant lag, it becomes $+3.5$ points (column 2): the raw shortfall reflects that domestic patents in emerging-market
offices are much less cited than foreign ones. Columns 3--6 estimate it separately for each owner group. Measured
against foreign owners in the same office, small emerging-market firms keep their patents as long as small
advanced-economy firms do (0.5 points), mid-sized and large firms longer, by 8.0 and 5.4 points, and universities
less long, by 14.2 points.
The robust result is that emerging-market firms are not behind.

\paragraph{Which offices drive it.} China and the United States are the largest offices on each side, so the lower
two blocks of Table~\ref{tab:survival} separate them. China against the United States alone gives a pooled estimate of
3.6, with Chinese small firms 2.6 points ahead, mid-sized firms 10.3 points ahead and universities 19.7 points behind.
Without China and the United States, small firms in Korea, Russia, Brazil and the Czech Republic are 4.8 points behind
small firms in Japan, Germany, the United Kingdom and France, while mid-sized and large firms are 5.1 and 1.7 points
ahead. The benchmark matters here: small Japanese firms' patents are 19 points more likely to be alive than foreign
owners' patents in Japan, by far the largest domestic advantage among the advanced-economy offices (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:within}).

\paragraph{Robustness.} The small-firm estimate stays within about a point of zero when foreign patents are reweighted
to the domestic citation distribution, when survival is counted from filing and when domestic and foreign owners pay
identical fees; mid-sized and large firms stay 2--9 points ahead (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rich}). Without the
grant-lag controls every estimate is 1--3 points higher, and a bound for selection on unobservables makes
emerging-market firms look better.

\paragraph{By country.} Appendix Table~\ref{tab:bycountry} compares each emerging-market office with the five
advanced-economy offices. Small firms are level in China (0.7), ahead in Korea (3.9) and behind in Russia ($-7.9$); mid-sized firms are 8--12
points ahead in China and Korea and behind in Russia, Brazil and the Czech Republic. Brazil, with about 500 domestic
firm patents, is measured imprecisely. Russia stands apart: for all owners, its domestic patents are 14 points behind. In China, universities, 19.7
points behind, offset most of domestic firms' lead for all owners together.

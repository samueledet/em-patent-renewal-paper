\section{Survival at equal quality}\label{sec:survival}

\paragraph{Main estimates.} Table~\ref{tab:survival} reports $\delta$ in equation~\eqref{eq:did}, the extra
domestic--foreign gap in emerging-market offices, for all firms and universities together. Across the ten offices,
without quality controls it is $-10.1$ (1.5) points: domestic patents in emerging-market offices are much less cited
than foreign ones (column 1). With office-specific citation slopes and field and owner-group effects it becomes $+4.1$
(1.4) (column 2). Table~\ref{tab:groups} breaks the estimate down by owner. Emerging-market firms of every size are
ahead of advanced-economy firms relative to foreign owners in the same office: small firms by 2.8 (0.5) points,
mid-sized firms by 7.9 (1.0) and large firms by 9.0 (2.6). Universities are behind, at $-5.7$ (3.6).

\paragraph{Which offices drive it.} China and the United States are the largest offices on each side, so
columns 3--6 of Table~\ref{tab:survival} and panels B and C of Table~\ref{tab:groups} separate them. China against the
United States alone gives the same pooled estimate, $+3.9$ (2.2), with Chinese small firms 5.4 (0.6) points ahead and
Chinese universities 15.3 (3.2) points behind. Without China and the United States the pooled estimate is close to
zero, $-1.0$ (1.5); mid-sized and large firms are still ahead, by 2.4 (1.4) and 3.5 (2.9) points, the second not
significantly, but small firms in Korea, Russia, Taiwan
and the Czech Republic are 4.4 (0.9) points behind small firms in Japan, Germany, the United Kingdom and France. The
benchmark matters here: small Japanese firms keep their home patents 18 points more often than foreign owners do in
Japan, by far the largest domestic advantage among the advanced-economy offices (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:within}), and
without the United States Japan sets the bar.

\paragraph{Richer quality controls.} Citations are a noisy measure of quality. Adding citations that block later
claims, citations received by age four and office-specific slopes in the grant lag lowers the estimates for every
firm size by 2--3 points (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rich}). Small emerging-market firms then come out level, at $-0.4$
(0.6), and 1.7 (0.7) points behind with family size, which itself partly reflects owners' capacity to file abroad;
mid-sized and large firms remain 4--8 points ahead.

\paragraph{By country.} Figure~\ref{fig:country} compares each emerging-market office with the five advanced-economy
offices, by owner group. Chinese and Korean firms of every size are ahead: small firms by 4.0 (0.6) and 5.1 (0.9)
points, larger firms by 7--11 points. Small Czech firms are ahead by a similar amount, 6.4 points, though with a wide
interval. Russia is the exception: small Russian firms are 4.3 (1.5) points behind, and Russian universities 11.9
(4.7). Small Taiwanese firms are 2.4 (1.4) points behind, while large Taiwanese firms and universities are well ahead.
Within each office, the domestic--foreign gap for small firms is close to zero in Russia and Taiwan, against 2--18
points in the advanced-economy offices (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:within}).

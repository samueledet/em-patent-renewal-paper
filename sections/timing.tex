\section{The timing artefact}\label{sec:timing}

\paragraph{Renewal fees start at grant, not at filing.} If two otherwise identical patents are filed on the same day
and one is granted two years earlier, by ten years from filing its owner has faced two more renewal decisions, and
the patent is more likely to have lapsed for that reason alone. Survival measured from filing therefore mixes value
with exposure whenever grant speeds differ between the groups being compared.

\paragraph{Emerging-market offices grant domestic patents years sooner.} Figure~\ref{fig:lags} shows the median grant
lag of small firms' patents by office. In four of the five emerging-market offices, domestic owners are granted
1.7--3.6 years sooner than foreign owners; in Taiwan the difference is 0.6 years. In the advanced-economy offices it
is a year or less, and in the United States and the United Kingdom domestic owners wait slightly longer. Many foreign
applications enter through the PCT national phase, and examination in the emerging-market offices was slower for
applications in foreign languages and with foreign priority claims.

\paragraph{Measured from grant, the gap disappears for firms.} Figure~\ref{fig:timing} estimates
equation~\eqref{eq:did} for each owner group with two outcomes. Measured as survival to ten years from filing, the
domestic shortfall in emerging-market offices is larger than in advanced-economy offices for small firms
(\tbd{x.x} points) and universities (\tbd{x.x}). Measured as survival to eight years after grant, it reverses for firms
of every size: relative to foreign owners in the same office, emerging-market firms keep their patents 1.7 (0.5)
points more often than advanced-economy firms if they are small, and 8--9 points more often if they are larger. Only
the university gap remains, at $-5.7$ (3.6). Pooling all groups, the extra gap moves from $-5.1$ (1.4) points from
filing to $+4.3$ (1.5) from grant (Table~\ref{tab:survival}). Adding blocking citations, early citations or family
size changes these estimates by less than a point (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rich}); adding the grant lag does almost
all the work.

\paragraph{The lesson is general.} Grant speeds differ across offices, owner nationalities, entry routes and periods.
Comparisons of patent survival, and of renewal-based values, should start the clock at grant or at the first renewal
fee. All results below do.
